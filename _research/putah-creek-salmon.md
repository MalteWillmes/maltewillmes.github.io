### Salmon in Putah Creek

Lower Putah Creek is a small, highly modified stream in California's Central Valley that flows from Lake Berryessa through the town of Winters and past the UC Davis campus before entering the Yolo Bypass. Following restoration of a more natural flow regime, adult Chinook Salmon (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) began returning regularly to the creek, accompanied by observations of spawning and juvenile production.

We use otoliths collected from adult salmon carcasses to reconstruct the age and natal origins of fish spawning in Putah Creek. Otoliths form continuous growth increments throughout a fish's life and preserve chemical signatures of the environments it occupied. In particular, spatial variation in ^87Sr/^86Sr among Central Valley rivers and hatcheries allows us to distinguish among potential natal sources and reconstruct the origins of returning adults.

Our initial work showed that salmon spawning in Putah Creek originated from multiple locations throughout California's Central Valley and were dominated by hatchery-origin strays. These results demonstrated how dispersing fall-run Chinook Salmon can rapidly colonize newly restored habitat.

Continued monitoring across subsequent spawning years provided evidence for the next stage of population establishment. By combining otolith ^87Sr/^86Sr, trace-element chemistry, coded-wire tags, age estimates, and juvenile monitoring, we identified adults whose chemical signatures were consistent with natal origin in Putah Creek. These fish provide evidence that salmon can successfully reproduce in the restored watershed, survive juvenile outmigration and the marine phase, and return as adults to spawn.

Putah Creek therefore provides a valuable natural experiment for understanding how salmon populations form in restored and previously underutilized habitats. The project highlights the interaction between straying, hatchery supplementation, habitat restoration, and natal homing during the early stages of population establishment.
