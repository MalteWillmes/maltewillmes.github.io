Modern monitoring provides only a short window into the life-history diversity of California Chinook Salmon. Archaeological fish remains provide a much longer record and can be used to place recent changes in a historical context.

Working with archaeological otoliths from the Feather River watershed, we reconstructed salmon return ages across several time periods spanning roughly four millennia. Current work continues to explore how archaeological and historical archives can provide ecological baselines for interpreting changes in salmon populations.
