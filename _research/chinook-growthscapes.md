Juvenile Chinook Salmon move through river, floodplain, and bypass habitats where growth conditions can vary strongly through space and time. Otolith growth increments provide a way to reconstruct individual size and growth trajectories after fish have left these habitats.

Building on experimental validation of otolith-based growth reconstructions, this project compares juvenile growth across Central Valley rearing grounds and environmental conditions. The aim is to better describe when and where different habitats provide favorable growth opportunities and how those patterns vary among years.
